A car-themed, self-hosted AI garage app with a free tier and a Pro plan (£5/month or £40/year). This file holds the full idea, the architecture, and 211 instructions to give to Claude one at a time.

## 1. The idea

Apex Garage is a web app (installable as a PWA on phone and desktop) for people who own and modify cars. Its centrepiece is "Crew Chief", an AI assistant that runs on your own home server through Ollama, so there are no per-token API costs.

### What users get

- **Garage:** a profile for each car (make, model, year, trim, mileage, photos, VIN, notes).
- **Build Log:** a timeline of mods, costs, dyno or lap results and before/after photos.
- **Maintenance:** service schedules, mileage and date reminders, receipts.
- **Parts Locker:** parts you own or plan to buy, with fitment notes and a budget tracker.
- **Crew Chief AI:** ask questions about their specific car ("what should I do before fitting coilovers?"), get plans from their build log, diagnose symptoms, and summarise service history.
- **Print Service:** upload a 3D model of a car part (STL or 3MF), pick a material, see an instant itemised quote for that exact part, pay, and have it printed and shipped. Prices come from the real filament weight plus a configurable markup (50% by default) and shipping. Includes safety screening and manual review of orders (see Phase 13).
- **Social and Journeys:** share photos of your cars or anything you've shot with friends and crews, record journeys (distance, time, average and max speed) and share them, with leaderboards designed to be safe (see Phase 12), plus branded share cards for Instagram and other apps.

### Honest constraints to plan around

- A single home PC serves only a few AI chats at once. The design uses a request queue and clear "you're in line" UX. Cap concurrent users and watch capacity before you advertise.
- Home internet means your uptime depends on your ISP and power. Start as a beta with honest expectations before charging people.
- Taking payments and storing personal data in the UK brings obligations (UK GDPR, ICO fee, consumer law, VAT thresholds). Get T&Cs and the privacy policy reviewed by a solicitor before launch. I'm not a lawyer.

## 2. Architecture

- **Frontend:** Next.js (React, TypeScript), Tailwind, Framer Motion, PWA. Static assets served through Cloudflare.
- **Mobile:** an Expo (React Native) companion app for reliable background GPS journey recording and push notifications. Phone browsers cannot track location reliably in the background, especially on iPhone, so a web-only version cannot do this properly. Maps use MapLibre.
- **Admin console:** a separate app (apps/admin) on its own hidden hostname, reachable only by you through Cloudflare Access and/or Tailscale, with passkey login, a central config store, feature flags, an audit log and a short list of approved remote actions (see Phase 14).
- **Backend:** Node.js with TypeScript (Fastify) or Python FastAPI. Pick one and stay with it.
- **Database:** PostgreSQL, with Redis for queues, rate limiting and sessions.
- **AI:** Ollama running a ~9B model on your PC, behind a queue worker. The Ollama port is never exposed to the internet.
- **Billing:** Stripe Checkout and Customer Portal with webhooks.
- **Storage:** local disk with encrypted off-site backups.
- **Hosting:** Docker Compose on the home server, reached only through Cloudflare Tunnel (no open router ports), with Tailscale for your own admin access.
- **Monitoring:** Uptime Kuma, Prometheus and Grafana, plus structured logs.

### Phase 14: Owner admin console (189-211)

This is your remote control panel. It is deliberately not a hidden login on the public site. ## 6. About the "10,000 optimisations" goal

A literal list of 10,000 items would be mostly padding, and no one could review it. A better way to get the same effect is a continuous improvement loop. After instruction 211, give Claude these prompts repeatedly, one category at a time:

- **A:** "Audit the [frontend | API | database | AI gateway | Docker/infra] for performance problems. List up to 25 specific issues with file paths, ranked by impact. Do not change anything yet."
- **B:** "Implement the top 10 from that list, with tests, one commit each."
- **C:** "Audit [accessibility | error handling | security | copy and UX | mobile layout] and list up to 25 specific issues. Do not change anything yet."
- **D:** "Implement the top 10 from that list, with tests."
- **E:** "Update docs/DECISIONS.md and the changelog."

Cycle through categories (performance, security, accessibility, UX polish, reliability, code quality, mobile, offline, SEO, observability). A few dozen rounds will produce hundreds of real, verified fixes, which is far better than an unreviewed pile of 10,000.

## 8. Before charging real customers

- Run a free private beta first and measure real AI capacity on your hardware.
- Get a solicitor to review the Terms and Privacy Policy.
- Check your ISP's terms allow running a public service from a home connection.
- Check your home insurance and any tax obligations for trading income.
- Work out the storage economics first. Cloud object storage typically costs a few pounds per terabyte per month, so a fully used 1 TB on a £5 plan would lose money once payment fees and VAT are taken off. On a home server the cost is drives, backups and upload bandwidth instead. Instruction 132 helps you track this.
- Free is now generous at 200 GB, so it is a real cost and an abuse risk. Even if only a fraction of free users upload anything, free accounts can quickly outnumber paying ones. Consider an inactivity rule in the Terms (for example, warn then remove Free files after 12 months of no logins), and use the waitlist in instruction 130 to cap total Free signups until you know your capacity.
- Max is the riskiest plan for storage. At £8 a month (or about £5.83 a month on the £70 yearly plan), a fully used 4 TB would cost more to store than the plan earns, so rely on instructions 122, 130 and 132 (quotas, capacity waitlist, cost dashboard) and review the 4 TB figure after the beta.
- Do not advertise "1 TB" or "4 TB" unless you can honestly deliver it to every Pro user at once. UK advertising and consumer rules expect the promise to match reality.
- Keep customer data minimal. The less you store, the less you can lose.
